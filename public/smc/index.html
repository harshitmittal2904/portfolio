/ 01 · The brief

Why I built this before applying.

SMC Global Securities — one of India's oldest broking houses, 35 years old, family-promoted, with 2,400+ sub-brokers across the country — posted a Senior PM role focused on broking platforms, eKYC, CRM, and partner ecosystems. The JD was wide; the company sits at an inflection point, trying to compete with Zerodha and Groww via its discount arm StoxKart, which has been in market since 2017.

Standard application material wouldn't tell them anything new about me. So I treated the application as a PM problem in itself: what's the smallest piece of real, original work I could produce that demonstrates I'd be useful from day one?

The answer, for a fintech PM role, was a focused product proposal addressing two of the five JD pillars — eKYC and partner ecosystem — with mockups, a 90-day plan, and KPI targets. The kind of artefact a hiring manager actually has time to read.

— The wedge hypothesis

StoxKart's site advertises "15-minute account opening." Public reviews suggest the reality is 24 hours to 4 working days. That gap is where the customer goes to Zerodha or Groww — both of whom solved this years ago.

The JD's eKYC pillar, taken alone, was the highest-leverage problem to attack. Fix that, and several of the JD's other pillars (CRM, partner ecosystem, lifecycle) become tractable as a consequence.

/ 02 · The research

The infrastructure is commoditised. The differentiation is product.

The most important insight from the research phase was counter-intuitive. Zerodha and Groww don't have a secret KYC pipeline. Every SEBI-registered broker uses the same six government-provided rails:

  • DigiLocker — Government of India, fetches digitally-signed Aadhaar XML
  • UIDAI Aadhaar OTP — direct identity verification (allowed since SEBI April 2020 circular)
  • PAN / NSDL — PAN database authenticity check
  • KRA / CKYC — central KYC repositories (CVL, CAMS, NSDL, Karvy)
  • IMPS Penny Drop — bank account verification via ₹1 transfer with name match
  • Aadhaar eSign — Digio / NSDL Instigo for legally-binding digital signature

What separates Zerodha's 5-minute account from SMC's 4-day account isn't infrastructure. It's product orchestration — how cleverly you stitch the rails together, how aggressively you parallelise the API calls, how high you set the auto-approval confidence threshold, and how mobile-native the UI feels. Every one of those is a PM problem.

"When the rails are commoditised, the moat isn't in the technology. It's in the product team that orchestrates them."

— What's actually new here

Steps 01-06 and the government rails layer are the same as Zerodha's. The differentiator is the AI Intervention Layer — the SK Coach on stages 02 and 05, and the SK Reviewer in backend. Each is a small, scoped piece of agentic UX that fires only when the model detects friction. None of these require new infrastructure. They're product orchestration on top of commodity rails.

/ 06 · The ops cockpit

External polish is half the work. The other half is what the ops team uses every day.

The second design surface was the internal KYC review console. Today, most broker ops teams review applications in spreadsheet-grade UIs — opening a customer record, reading 10 fields, cross-referencing three vendor dashboards, making a manual call. That workflow sets the ceiling on how fast accounts can be approved.

The redesign flips the model: the AI does the cross-referencing first. It surfaces a one-paragraph summary, a confidence score, and a recommendation. The reviewer becomes a checker on the AI's work, not a primary decider. Real-time funnel data sits below the queue, so the ops team is always aware of where customers are dropping today, not last quarter.

— Honest framing

These targets are illustrative, not measured. They reflect what's plausibly recoverable from current StoxKart funnel benchmarks within two quarters. In any conversation with the company, they'd be framed as "based on industry data, this is the size of the prize — the first job is to instrument and find out the real numbers." Overclaiming is the fastest way to lose credibility in a fintech interview.

/ 07 · The plan

What I'd ship in 90 days.

The proposal closed with a sequenced 90-day plan. The principle: ship a measurable win in the first month before redesigning anything. Most candidates rush to mockups; the right answer is to instrument first, lift the obvious thresholds, and let the data point at the redesign priorities.

Days 0-30
Diagnose

Instrument first, change nothing

Instrument the existing eKYC funnel end-to-end in Mixpanel or Amplitude. Most broking platforms don't have stage-level drop-off in a single dashboard. Pull the last 90 days of failed onboardings, cluster the failure modes, shadow five sub-brokers and five ops agents to map the real workflow vs the documented one. Ship: a single funnel dashboard plus the top three friction hypotheses, ranked by recoverable revenue.

Days 30-60
Ship the Coach

Lift thresholds. Kill redundancies. Coach Stage 1.

Lift auto-approval confidence thresholds (a free win — most slowness is conservative defaults, not real risk). Audit every screen against "if DigiLocker just gave us this data, why are we asking for it again?" Stage 1 of the AI Coach goes live on the highest friction point — likely Aadhaar OTP or Video KYC. Branch / Video-KYC fallback flow integrated with SMC's existing branch network — turning a moat into a feature.

Days 60-90
Scale + partner

Roll the Coach. Ship the Reviewer. Partner cockpit MVP.

Roll the Coach to all stages. Ship the Ops KYC Reviewer to deflect ~40% of the manual review queue. Partner cockpit MVP for top 200 sub-brokers — live lead status, AI nudges, near-real-time commission visibility. Compress payout cycle from 30-45 days towards 7-day rolling. By day 90, the lead-to-active pipeline for the sub-broker channel goes from "weeks of friction" to "hand the phone to the customer."

/ 08 · Takeaways

What this exercise actually taught me.

1. Acquisition and conversion are one problem.

The cleanest insight from the research was that fixing onboarding without fixing acquisition wastes leads, and fixing acquisition without fixing onboarding has nothing to convert. SMC's specific shape is that both are broken — the sub-broker channel brings hot leads, then 24-72 hour activation kills intent, then sub-brokers stop bothering. A PM with both fixes can move the needle in a single quarter. That's a more interesting position than "fix the funnel."

2. Domain fluency > domain experience.

I haven't worked in broking. The closest pattern in my background is the Nutrabay marketplace seller platform — automated KYC, commission engine, NLP compliance, seller scoring. Different regulator, identical architecture. The vocabulary changes; the product problem doesn't. A weekend of focused research closed enough of the domain gap to have a credible point of view.

3. The application IS the work sample.

For PM roles especially, traditional cover letters and resumes tell the hiring manager almost nothing about how you actually think. A small artefact built specifically for their company tells them everything. The resume becomes corroboration. The proposal becomes the actual evaluation surface.

4. Three audiences, three drafts.

The recruiter, the founder, and the future me each needed a completely different framing of the same idea. Conflating them — sending the founder-level deep dive to a recruiter, or the recruiter-level summary to a founder — is what makes most outreach miss.

"Don't claim experience you can't defend. Don't pretend insider knowledge you don't have. Demonstrate the rigour of your thinking, not the depth of your access."
