Why this works

The flow respects three principles. One: the government does the verification — DigiLocker, KRA, IMPS, Aadhaar eSign — so the user does almost no typing. Two: screen 2 (the KRA pre-fill) is the conversion lever no one talks about — most users have KYC'd somewhere before, and the right product turns that into a moment of delight. Three: the AI Coach on Video KYC isn't a chatbot; it's a contextual nudge layer that fires only when the model detects friction. That's the differentiator that compounds.

What changes for the ops team

Today, an ops reviewer opens an application and reads through 10 fields, cross-references three vendor dashboards, and makes a manual call. In the new console, the AI has already done the cross-referencing — it surfaces a one-paragraph summary, a confidence score, and a recommendation. The reviewer becomes a checker on the AI's work, not a primary decider. Throughput goes up 4-5x. The funnel panel below the queue keeps the team always-aware of where customers are dropping today, in real time. That awareness alone changes the conversation in the standup.

/ Closing

What these screens are not.

These are not final designs. They're directional artefacts — built to make the proposal real, to give Shruti or Pranay something to react to in a 15-minute conversation, and to demonstrate that the work has been thought about at the screen level, not just at the strategy level.

Every screen is built to be defensible: the KRA pre-fill is real (CKYC + the four KRAs are public infrastructure). The DigiLocker tap is real (it's how Zerodha and Groww do it). The Video KYC OTP card is exactly how Zerodha runs IPV today. The AI Coach is the one piece that's genuinely new — and it's small enough to ship in a quarter.

If the founders ask "could we actually build this?" — the honest answer is yes, and most of it is just product orchestration of infrastructure that already exists.
